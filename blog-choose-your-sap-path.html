SAP Strategy

ECC, S/4HANA on-premise, private or public cloud: how to choose your SAP path

Frontino IT October 2026 3 min read

Every company running SAP ECC faces the same question: where to go next, and when. The answer is not the same for everyone, and the market is not only about public cloud. Here is a practical way to look at the options.

The deadline that frames the decision

SAP's mainstream maintenance for SAP ECC 6.0 (enhancement packages 6 to 8) ends on 31 December 2027. Optional extended maintenance is available until the end of 2030, at additional cost. Under RISE with SAP, a transition option can stretch support to 2033, but only for selected customers that meet specific conditions, so it is not a general extension. A move to S/4HANA takes time to plan and deliver, so for most companies the decision belongs on the agenda now, even if the go-live is years away.

The main options

  • SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition (typically through GROW with SAP). A standardised, SAP-managed SaaS ERP with regular upgrades. Fastest to adopt, and best suited to companies that can work with standard processes.
  • SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition (typically through RISE with SAP). More flexibility and room for specific requirements, with infrastructure and operations managed in the cloud.
  • SAP S/4HANA on-premise. Full control over the system, the infrastructure and the upgrade schedule, with the responsibility for running it.
  • Staying on ECC for now. A valid short-term choice if you use the time well: cleaning up custom code, modernising integrations and preparing data.

Five questions that decide the right path

  1. How standard are your processes? If your differentiation lies in products and service rather than in ERP processes, public cloud becomes very attractive.
  2. How much custom code do you rely on? Heavy, business-critical customisation points to private cloud or on-premise, at least as a first step.
  3. What does your IT team want to run? Public cloud removes most of the operational work. On-premise keeps it in-house.
  4. How fast do you need to move? Standard-first public cloud projects can go live much faster than full transformations.
  5. How complex is your integration landscape? Many interfaces call for an integration strategy, usually on SAP BTP, whatever the deployment model.
Whatever the path, the principles are the same. Keep the core clean, build extensions and integrations on SAP BTP, and treat data quality as part of the project rather than an afterthought.

Start with facts, not with a product

The best decisions start from an honest picture of your current landscape: processes, custom code, interfaces and data. A short, structured readiness assessment gives you that picture, and a clear recommendation, before you commit budget to a full programme.
